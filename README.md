# Japanese Learning Hub — User Guide

## What this app is

Japanese Learning Hub is a private study dashboard for working systematically through Japanese textbooks.

Your current programme is:

**TOBIRA Beginning Japanese II · 12-week foundation**

The app separates:

- **Books** — the actual learning resources
- **Programmes** — plans for studying those resources
- **Lessons / sections** — the structure of the books
- **Tasks** — the specific work you do
- **Progress / notes** — your personal study record

The goal is to make the dashboard tell you **what to study, where to find it in the book, and what to do with it**.

---

# 1. Logging in

Open the deployed site.

If you are not logged in, the app only shows the private login screen.

Click:

**Login → enter your Supabase email and password → Sign in**

After successful authentication, the dashboard becomes available.

Your study progress and notes are stored in Supabase, so they can persist across devices when you use the same account.

## Logging out

Use **Logout** in the top-right account area.

---

# 2. Dashboard

The Dashboard is the main landing page.

It is intended to answer:

> **What should I be doing now?**

Use it to see:

- your current programme
- the current week
- today's study
- progress through the programme
- the next task
- supporting/optional study

The core programme is based around approximately **15–20 hours of Japanese study per week**.

The textbook/workbook work is the priority. Supporting resources such as Migaku, WaniKani, shadowing and Japanese media are additional study rather than requirements for every day.

---

# 3. Study Plan

Open **Study Plan** from the navigation.

This is the main place to work through the 12-week programme.

Each week is divided into study days.

A typical week distributes the different TOBIRA components rather than asking you to complete an entire lesson in one sitting.

You will see tasks for areas such as:

- Conversation
- Vocabulary
- Kanji
- Grammar
- Activities / 話しましょう
- Reading / 読みましょう
- Listening / 聞きましょう
- Workbook practice
- Review / catch-up

## How to use a task

Click a task.

The task window gives you:

1. **What to study**
2. **Which book/resource to use**
3. **The exact page reference**
4. **The section of the lesson**
5. **Suggested study workflow**
6. **A place for your notes**
7. **Completion status**

The intention is that you should be able to go directly from the app to the physical book without guessing.

For example, instead of simply seeing:

> Lesson 11 Grammar

you should see a reference such as:

> TOBIRA Beginning Japanese II · Textbook · pp.25–34

# 4. Working through TOBIRA Beginning Japanese II

The current programme covers **Lessons 11–20**.

The textbook is organised into:

- Conversation
- Vocabulary
- Kanji
- Grammar
- Activities
- Reading
- Listening

The app follows this structure.

## Recommended workflow

When a textbook section is assigned:

### Conversation

Use the textbook/audio to:

1. Listen.
2. Read and understand the conversation.
3. Listen again.
4. Repeat/practise the dialogue.
5. Pay attention to the conversation strategy identified by TOBIRA.

### Vocabulary

Work from context first where practical, then use the vocabulary list.

Aim for recognition and retrieval rather than simply rereading the list.

### Kanji

Work through the new kanji, readings and compounds.

Then use the relevant Workbook 1 practice.

### Grammar

Work through each grammar point in the textbook.

For each important point:

1. Understand the form and meaning.
2. Study the textbook examples.
3. Make your own examples.
4. Complete the corresponding Workbook 2 practice.
5. Make a note of anything that remains uncertain.

### Activities / 話しましょう

These are production activities.

Do them actively rather than treating them as reading exercises.

Speak your answers aloud and produce your own Japanese.

### Reading

Follow the reading strategy provided by TOBIRA.

Work through:

1. Preparation/context
2. Main reading
3. Comprehension
4. Structure/vocabulary
5. Summary or response where appropriate

### Listening

Listen before relying on the written material where practical.

Use repeated listening to identify what you missed, then replay and shadow where useful.

# 6. Completing a task

When you finish a task:

1. Open the task.
2. Check **Completed**.
3. Add notes if useful.
4. Close/save the task.

Completion is the main progress measure.

You do **not** need to use the mastery system for everything.

Mastery/confidence fields are available if you find them useful, but they are intentionally secondary to simply working through the curriculum.

---

# 7. Notes

There are two useful levels of notes.

## General study notes

Use the notes area for broader observations such as:

- recurring grammar problems
- things you want to investigate
- study reflections
- patterns you're noticing

## Task notes

Use notes inside an individual task for specific issues.

For example:

> I keep confusing these two grammar patterns.

or:

> Couldn't produce this sentence naturally — review later.

Task notes stay associated with that piece of study.

---

# 8. Books / Learning Hub

Open **Books** to see the resources in the Learning Hub.

A book is separate from a programme.

For example:

**TOBIRA Beginning Japanese II**

is a book.

**TOBIRA Beginning II · 12-week foundation**

is a programme using that book.

This distinction allows you to add future books without automatically changing your current study plan.

## Book detail

Click a book to open its detailed view.

You can see:

- book information
- associated resources
- units
- lessons
- sections
- page references
- associated programme/progress

The intention is that the Learning Hub eventually becomes a library of your Japanese-learning resources rather than being tied specifically to TOBIRA.

---

# 9. Pomodoro

Click the **🍅 Pomodoro** button in the navigation.

The timer is intentionally hidden until you need it, keeping the study dashboard uncluttered.

Use it when you want a focused study block.

The Pomodoro is a study aid, not a separate curriculum task.

On mobile, the Pomodoro is designed to remain contained within the screen.

---

# 10. Suggested daily routine

A practical session can look like this:

### Start

Open the Dashboard.

Check:

> **Today's study**

Open the first task.

### Study

Get the referenced book and pages.

Follow the task's suggested workflow.

Use the Pomodoro if useful.

### Record

When finished:

- mark the task complete
- write a short note if something needs attention

### Continue

Move to the next task.

Don't spend unnecessary time maintaining the dashboard. The purpose of the app is to get you studying the books.

# 11. What counts as optional

The current system distinguishes between core and additional study.

## Core

Prioritise:

- TOBIRA textbook
- Workbook 1
- Workbook 2
- assigned reading/listening/writing/speaking

## Supporting

Useful for reinforcement:

- Migaku
- WaniKani
- shadowing
- Anki

## Optional input

Flexible Japanese exposure:

- YouTube
- manga
- drama
- podcasts
- novels

If you are short on time, **do the core TOBIRA work first**.

# 13. How future books will work

The Learning Hub is designed so that adding another textbook follows this process:

**Upload book → analyse contents → create book map → map sections/pages → add companion resources → create programme → schedule tasks**

A new book does not have to become an active programme immediately.

For example, a future TOBIRA book can first exist in the Books library while its contents are being analysed.

Only after the content map is ready should we create its study programme.

---

# 14. Important rule for using the app

Treat the **physical textbook/workbook as the source material**.

The dashboard is the organiser.

When the app says:

> Textbook · pp.25–34

open those pages and work through the actual TOBIRA material.

The app's study instructions tell you **how to approach the material**; they are not intended to replace the textbook.

---

# 15. If something looks wrong

Before changing anything in Supabase, check:

1. Are you logged into the correct account?
2. Is the correct programme selected?
3. Is the task showing the expected book/page?
4. Has the task already been completed?
5. Has the browser refreshed its session?

Do not rerun the database SQL just because the curriculum or UI has been updated. The current curriculum changes are designed to preserve the existing task IDs and progress data.
